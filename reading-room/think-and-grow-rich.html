WHY EL CHOSE THIS BOOK

Examining the Power of Our Thinking

The way we think can influence the goals we pursue, the decisions we make, and how we respond to challenges. But thinking alone does not create results.

In Think and Grow Rich, Napoleon Hill explores desire, belief, purpose, persistence, and other ideas he associates with achievement.

We are reading this book not to accept every claim as truth, but to examine its ideas thoughtfully. Which principles are useful? Which deserve questioning? And how might we apply what we learn responsibly?

BEFORE YOU READ

Examine Your Starting Point

Before exploring Napoleon Hill's ideas, take a moment to consider your own understanding of mindset, purpose, and personal growth.

01 — MINDSET
What beliefs do you currently hold about your ability to learn, grow, and change? Where did those beliefs come from?

02 — PURPOSE
How do you distinguish a purpose that genuinely matters to you from an ambition shaped by other people's expectations?

03 — PERSONAL GROWTH
What does personal growth mean to you beyond achievements, recognition, or material success?

You don't need perfect answers. Keep your reflections and revisit them after reading the book.

READ WITH INTENTION

Four Ideas Worth Examining

As you read, pay attention to how Napoleon Hill connects thought, belief, purpose, and achievement. Consider both the value and the limitations of his arguments.

01 — Desire

Hill places strong emphasis on having a definite desire. Consider how desire differs from a passing wish. Can desire provide direction without becoming an obsession?

02 — Faith and Autosuggestion

Hill argues that repeated thoughts and beliefs can influence achievement. Examine this claim carefully. How might self-belief influence behaviour, and where are its limitations?

03 — Specialised Knowledge

Hill distinguishes general knowledge from knowledge directed toward a particular purpose. What makes knowledge useful? Is possessing information enough, or does its application matter?

04 — Persistence

Hill presents persistence as important to achievement. But persistence also requires judgement. How can we distinguish worthwhile perseverance from continuing with an approach that no longer works?

Read critically: An author's confidence in an idea is not evidence that the idea is universally true. Consider personal circumstances, available opportunities, and the evidence behind claims about success.

REFLECT & APPLY

From Reading to Understanding

A book can introduce us to new perspectives, but understanding requires more than agreement. Consider what you have learned, what you question, and what deserves further examination.

01 — QUESTION
Which of Hill's arguments did you find least convincing, and why? What evidence would help you evaluate it?

02 — RECONSIDER
Did the book change your understanding of the relationship between mindset, purpose, and personal growth? If so, how?

03 — DISTINGUISH
Which ideas seem useful regardless of financial success, and which depend on circumstances beyond individual control?

A Small Experiment

Choose one principle from the book that you consider useful.

Over the next seven days, test it through one small, realistic action. Keep a brief record of what you did, what happened, and what you learned.

Final thought: What did your experience teach you about the principle's usefulness or limitations?
