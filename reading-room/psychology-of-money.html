EL QUIET READING ROOM

MONEY & FINANCIAL THINKING

The Psychology of Money

by Morgan Housel

A guided reading experience for thinking more deeply about your relationship with money, the beliefs and habits that influence your decisions, and the financial direction you want to build.

WHY EL CHOSE THIS BOOK

Why EL Chose This Book

I chose this book because I want you to understand your current financial position and your relationship with money.

It can help you become more self-aware about how you manage your money and how your experiences, beliefs and habits influence the financial decisions you make.

The purpose is not simply to learn more about money, but to examine how you think about it, how you behave with it, and the direction your financial decisions are taking you.

BEFORE YOU READ

Before You Read

Before opening this book, take a moment to notice where you currently stand in your relationship with money.

You may already have strong financial habits and knowledge, or you may still be figuring some things out. Either way, begin with an honest picture of what you currently think, practise and understand.

What do you already understand well about managing money?

What financial habits or decisions are you proud of?

What would you like to understand more deeply, question, strengthen or approach differently?

What are you curious to discover from this book?

Keep these thoughts in mind as you read. At the end, return to them and ask yourself: What was reinforced, what was challenged, and what changed?

READ WITH INTENTION

Ideas to Think About While Reading

As you read The Psychology of Money, don't focus only on finishing the book. Pay attention to the ideas that reinforce what you already know, challenge what you believe, or make you curious enough to think more deeply.

Your Money Beliefs

What has shaped the way you think about money? Which beliefs still make sense to you, and which deserve to be questioned?

Your Financial Habits

What behaviours have helped you manage money well? Which habits might need strengthening, changing or replacing?

Your Financial Decisions

What tends to influence your decisions: your priorities, emotions, past experiences, other people, long-term goals, or something else?

Your Definition of Enough

What does “enough” mean to you, and how does that definition influence the way you earn, save, spend or pursue wealth?

Your Long-Term Direction

Are your financial decisions supporting only your present needs, or are they also helping you build toward the future you want?

What You Disagree With

Is there an idea in the book you don't agree with? Why? What experiences, knowledge or reasoning support your position?

You don't have to accept every idea simply because it appears in a book. The purpose is to read, question, compare, reflect and form your own understanding.

REFLECT — DISCOVER

Reflection

Now that you've finished reading, pause before deciding what to change. First, notice what the reading helped you understand about yourself and your relationship with money.

How would you describe your relationship with money now that you've finished the book?

What did you believe about money before reading this book, and what do you believe now?

What did you discover about the experiences, beliefs or habits that influence your financial decisions?

What idea challenged you, surprised you, or reinforced something you already believed? Why?

REBUILD — DECIDE

Rebuild

Awareness gives you something to work with. Now decide what deserves to remain, what needs strengthening, and what may need to change.

What money belief do you want to keep, strengthen, reconsider or leave behind?

Which financial habit is already serving you well and deserves to continue?

Which habit or behaviour is working against the financial direction you want to take?

Where do you need a clearer financial boundary?

How do you want to become more intentional about the way you spend and manage your money?

Rebuilding does not always mean starting again. Sometimes it means protecting what already works, strengthening what is weak, changing what no longer serves you, and building what is missing.

APPLY — PRACTISE

30-Day Money Practice

A decision becomes meaningful when it begins to influence what you repeatedly do.

For the next 30 days, use what you identified in your Rebuild section to practise a more deliberate approach to your money.

01

PLAN

At the beginning of each week — or whenever you receive money — decide what your money needs to do before you begin spending it.

02

NURTURE

Choose one financial habit from your Rebuild section that you want to develop or strengthen. Focus on practising it consistently for the next 30 days.

03

TRACK

Keep a simple record of what you actually do. Pay attention to your spending, saving and financial decisions so you can compare your intentions with your behaviour.

04

REVIEW

At the end of each week, look back at what happened.

  • Did I follow the plan I made?
  • Where did I make deliberate decisions?
  • Where did I act out of habit or impulse?
  • What will I adjust next week?

At the end of 30 days:

What evidence can you see that your financial behaviour has changed, strengthened or become more intentional?

BECOME — TAKE IT WITH YOU

My Quiet Takeaway

You came here to read, but reading alone is not the rebuild.

Take a moment to think about what this book has revealed about your relationship with money — your beliefs, your habits, your decisions, and the direction you want to take from here.

What will you carry with you from this book?

What will you begin doing differently?

Let your answer become more than a thought. Take one deliberate action and allow what you have learned to become part of how you live.
