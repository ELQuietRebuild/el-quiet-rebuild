WHY EL CHOSE THIS BOOK

Why EL Chose This Book

Learning something and communicating it effectively are two different skills.

Sometimes we understand an idea but struggle to explain it clearly. Other times, we communicate something important, but the message doesn't stay with the person listening.

Made to Stick explores what makes certain ideas understandable, memorable and meaningful.

Through this reading experience, we'll examine how we organise our thoughts, communicate what we've learned and practise making our ideas clearer.

BEFORE YOU READ

Before You Read

Before exploring the ideas in this book, take a moment to examine how you currently understand, organise and communicate information.

You may already communicate clearly in some situations and struggle in others. The purpose of these questions is not to judge your ability, but to understand your starting point.

When you learn something new, how do you usually explain it to another person?

Have you ever understood an idea but struggled to express it clearly? What made it difficult?

What helps you remember an explanation, lesson or message?

When someone does not understand what you're explaining, how do you usually respond?

What would you like to improve about the way you communicate your ideas?

Write down your answers before you begin reading. You can return to them later to examine what you have learned and whether your approach to communication has changed.

READ WITH INTENTION

Ideas to Think About While Reading

As you read Made to Stick, pay attention to how the authors explain why some ideas are easier to understand, remember and communicate than others.

Use these six principles as a guide for observing how ideas are presented. You do not need to master everything immediately. Begin by noticing how each principle works.

01 — Simplicity

What is the central message? Can you remove unnecessary details without losing the meaning of the idea?

02 — Unexpectedness

What makes an idea surprising or interesting? How can curiosity help someone pay attention to an important message?

03 — Concreteness

Is the idea explained using specific examples, familiar situations or observable details? What makes it easier to understand?

04 — Credibility

Why should someone believe this message? What evidence, experience or trustworthy information supports it?

05 — Emotion

Why should the audience care about this idea? Does the message connect with something meaningful to them?

06 — Stories

How does a story help communicate an idea? What can someone understand through a real-life example that a definition alone might not explain?

You do not have to agree with every idea in a book. Read carefully, question what you learn, examine the examples and develop your own understanding.

REFLECT — DISCOVER

Reflection: What Did You Discover?

After reading Made to Stick, take time to examine what you have learned about communicating ideas. Return to the answers you wrote before reading.

Which of the six principles changed the way you think about communication? Why?

What did you discover about the way you currently explain your ideas?

Which example from the book helped you understand a principle more clearly?

Was there an idea you disagreed with or wanted to question further? What was your reasoning?

What would you like to communicate more effectively in your own life?

Reflection helps you recognise what you have understood, what you still need to examine, and where your learning could become useful.

REBUILD — DECIDE

Rebuild Your Communication Habits

Understanding what makes an idea memorable is useful. But understanding alone does not automatically improve the way we communicate.

Think about your current communication habits. Which ones already help you express yourself clearly? Which ones might need strengthening or changing?

What communication habit is already serving you well and deserves to continue?

Do you sometimes include too many details when explaining an idea? How could you make your message simpler?

Do you use clear examples when explaining unfamiliar or complicated information?

How do you usually establish credibility when sharing information?

Which of the six principles would you like to practise more intentionally? Why?

Choose one communication habit to strengthen and one principle from the book to practise. Begin with a manageable change rather than trying to improve everything at once.

APPLY — PRACTISE

The One-Minute Explanation Challenge

Choose one idea you've recently learned. Explain it clearly enough for someone unfamiliar with the topic to understand.

01 — EXPLAIN
Explain your idea in one minute.

02 — IMPROVE
Identify the central message and add one concrete example.

03 — TRY AGAIN
Explain it again. Is your message clearer?

Takeaway: What will you do differently the next time you communicate an important idea?
